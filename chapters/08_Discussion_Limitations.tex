\section{Discussion and Limitations}

\subsection{Applicability Conditions}
\label{sec:discussion-drift}

SAKI applies when co-located RocksDB instances share a fixed background budget,
write pressure drifts at window time scales, and the host can observe
tenant-local demand and maintenance symptoms. The evidence supports a narrow
systems claim: a host-level rank-and-assign controller can place an existing
compaction/I/O budget better than equal sharing under these conditions, without
oracle labels or extra capacity.

The bounded-drift condition matters because previous-window observations must
remain relevant to the next window. The main workload moves one tenant per
20-second window; \fastdrift pushes to 1.5 tenants/window and still improves
P99 and throughput, but its wide P999 interval exposes the expected boundary:
newly high-pressure tenants can be hit before the next assignment promotes
them. Abrupt reversals, sub-window bursts, and within-window phase changes may
outrun the controller. The 20-second window, 4/8/4 split, and concrete budgets
are mechanism-isolation settings; Section~\ref{sec:saki-design}'s conservation
rule supports other tier counts, budget spreads, or a continuous allocator. At
32/64 tenants, the controller remains live and tracks high sets, but
tail-latency behavior needs tier-spread calibration and platform isolation from
CPU and scheduling contention.

\subsection{Deployment Assumptions}

The continuous harness changes background rate limits through \ratecall. The
measured rate-limiter coverage microstudy shows this knob shapes
flush/compaction I/O in our configuration, so foreground latency and
throughput changes arise indirectly from maintenance service rather than
direct foreground throttling. RocksDB's built-in auto-tuned rate limiter
remains useful local machinery, but it adapts within per-tenant ceilings;
SAKI supplies the host-level loop that moves unused budget across tenants. A
natural deployment variant is to let SAKI choose tenant ceilings while RocksDB
autotuning adapts inside each ceiling; we did not evaluate that combined
policy.

A production deployment would need tenant inventory, periodic RocksDB
statistics, a conserved host budget, and per-tenant runtime actuation. Offered
demand should be measured before backpressure or retries hide pressure, and
operators would likely add smoothing, hysteresis, cooldowns, dwell times, and
SLO-aware floors. If metrics are incomplete, the safe fallback is all-mid
sharing. Deployments should treat the reference key as one demand-aware
ordering, normalize local signals, and bound residual terms while preserving
budget conservation and demand observability. Other LSM engines need analogous
tenant-scoped maintenance signals and a runtime maintenance-budget knob.

\subsection{Resource and Fairness Boundaries}

SAKI controls one resource dimension: background I/O budget placement. CPU,
cache, memory, device queueing, and per-level write amplification may dominate
elsewhere, especially when limiter traffic stops tracking maintenance output or
other metrics saturate while compaction debt remains high. A full
maintenance-cost decomposition is a separate multi-resource control problem.

The policy is also a targeted reallocation: shifting budget to high-pressure
tenants can cost lower-pressure tenants. The measured LOW-tier costs and the
4\,KB-value P999 boundary motivate SLO-aware floors and fairness policy around
the same fixed-budget loop. Scan-heavy workloads, range-scan SLOs, and
read-dominant deployments require their own ranking and validation layer.

\subsection{Trace Validation Boundary}

The public-trace audits define the conditions required for meaningful
controller validation. CacheLib validates trace-shaped replay but creates too
little RocksDB maintenance stress, while Baleen often has too few active write
tenants per window to actuate the fixed all-mid budget. The next validation
layer needs storage-engine traces with tenant identity, drift, and actuatable
supply under the same fixed-budget contract; non-leveled compaction styles and
joint multi-resource control are natural extensions once that condition is met.
